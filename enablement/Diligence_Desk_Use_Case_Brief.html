Executive summary

Every upmarket deal brings a security questionnaire

Most companies in the OneValley portfolio sell B2B software to banks, universities, government agencies, and enterprises. Each of those buyers sends a vendor security questionnaire before signing: questions on encryption, certifications, data retention, subprocessors, and incident response. The answers already exist in the company’s own policies, but finding and drafting them pulls a founder or engineering lead off the product for hours, and a slow or inconsistent answer stalls the deal.

Diligence Desk drafts those answers from the company’s customer-shareable trust documents. Each question gets a drafted answer, a status (Yes, Partial, No, or Not covered), a short verbatim evidence phrase, and the source section. When the documents are silent, the answer is routed to a named owner. A person approves every row before it leaves the company.

What makes this different

01

Evidence on every row

Each answer quotes the company’s own document and names the section, so the reviewer verifies in seconds instead of re-researching.

02

Not covered instead of a guess

When the documents are silent, the agent says so and routes the question to a named owner. A certification in progress is never answered as held.

03

Isolation by design

Each company’s documents live in their own store, and each OneValley customer gets its own Org. Every build is tested for cross-company mix-ups.

04

A person on every send

Every output ends with the questions that need a person and a reminder that drafts are not a compliance determination.

2. Where Diligence Desk adds value

The three use cases share one engine and one recipe. Each turns a company’s own trust documents into cited, reviewable answers, and none requires a change to how the company runs its product.

4
2.1

Portfolio founders answering enterprise buyers

The challenge

A seed or Series A company selling to a bank receives a 40 to 100 question security questionnaire. The founder or CTO spends hours hunting through policies and old answers while the deal clock runs, and there is no compliance team to hand it to.

What Seekr does

The founder names the company and pastes the questions. The agent searches only that company’s trust documents and returns one cited, statused row per question, with a short list of what only a person can answer.

The efficiency outcome

The work moves from hunting to judging. Pilot target, to validate: a complete first draft in under 30 minutes and more than 80% of rows accepted without edits.

2.2

OneValley’s own procurement questionnaires

The challenge

OneValley sells to universities and government organizations, and each deal brings its own IT vendor assessment. The same questions come back on every deal, and engineering time goes to answering them.

What Seekr does

The same agent runs on OneValley’s own trust documents. Engineering answers only the questions flagged as needing a person.

The efficiency outcome

Faster procurement responses with an evidence trail on every answer, and a live reference OneValley can show its own customers.

5
2.3

Operators delivering it as a service

The challenge

OneValley operators need services they can stand up for each portfolio company without engineering, inside the support model OneValley already runs.

What Seekr does

A step-by-step build guide and a tested data kit. An operator creates a store for the company, reuses the tool and agent settings, and runs a short answer-key test that includes one deliberate gap.

The efficiency outcome

One afternoon to build and test once the company’s Org exists, which takes 24 to 72 hours. Build questions stay with OneValley as L1 and L2 support.

Recommended next step

Run a two-week pilot with one portfolio company that has an active enterprise deal. We need its four customer-shareable trust documents (security policy, data handling standard, subprocessor list, and continuity summary), one recent questionnaire, and a named reviewer. We measure minutes to first draft, rows accepted without edits, gaps surfaced, and turnaround against the company’s current baseline.

We would welcome a working session with OneValley to choose the pilot company and agree the success criteria.
